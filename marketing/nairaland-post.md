## Post — Technology Market

**Title:** I Built A Free Chrome Extension That Records What You Actually Did At Work (No Sign-up, No Card)

Good day, Nairalanders.

I built a Chrome extension called Tasker and I am sharing it here for anyone who needs it.
It is free, and I am the developer.

**The problem it solves**

Appraisal time comes, or a client asks for a breakdown of hours, or you are updating your
CV after two years on a job — and you cannot remember what you actually did. You spent the
period working seriously, but the evidence is gone. So you write something vague and you
undersell yourself.

**What it does**

Tasker sits in Chrome and records the time you spend on each site while you work. It sorts
that time into categories (development, research, communication, design, and others),
scores your day, and turns each day into a simple written log. At the end of the month it
produces a full recap — total hours worked, how many days you were active, where the time
went, and a short written summary of the month.

You do not start any timer. You just work, and it keeps the record.

**Why I think it fits people here**

- **It is completely free. No sign-up, no account, no credit card.** Most serious time
  trackers charge in dollars every month and want an international card. I know that is a
  wall for plenty of people here, so there is no payment wall at all — there is no account
  system in the software to charge you through.
- **If you work remotely or freelance for foreign clients**, you can invoice from a record
  that was kept while the work happened, instead of estimating hours from memory.
- **If you are in paid employment**, you walk into your appraisal with a month-by-month
  record of what you delivered instead of trying to recall January in December.
- **It is light on data.** The tracking happens on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded
  as you browse, so it does not sit there eating your subscription in the background.
  Tracking also keeps working when your network is down.

**On privacy, since it is a tracker**

Your browsing record stays on your own computer, in the browser's local storage. There is
no account and no analytics. Two things are optional and off unless you choose them:
backing up your logs to your own Google Drive, and a short AI-written summary of the month
which sends category totals only ("development: 58 hours") and never your website
addresses, page titles, or notes. You can also block any site from being tracked at all —
banking, email, health — with one click.

**What it does not do**

It only sees what happens inside Chrome. Work you do in Word, on calls, or on your phone
is invisible to it. If most of your work is not in a browser, it will undercount you.

**Where to get it**

https://tasker-landing-liard.vercel.app/

That page has the install button and the full privacy policy. It is live on the Chrome Web
Store, so it installs in one click.

I built this alone and I am still improving it. If you try it, please come back and tell me
what is wrong with it — especially if it puts a website in the wrong category, or if the
daily score does not match how your day actually went. That feedback is worth more to me
than downloads.

Thank you.

## Replies to expect

**"Is it really free? What is the catch?"**
> Genuinely free. There is no account system in it, so there is nothing to upgrade you into
> and no card stored anywhere. The only thing that costs me money is the optional monthly AI
> summary, which is why that part is rate-limited.

**"So you are collecting our browsing history?"**
> It is recorded, but it stays on your own machine — there is no server of mine holding it,
> because the extension has no backend. That is also why there is no account. If you do not
> want a site recorded at all, add it to the excluded list and it is never touched.

**"Does it work on Firefox / on phone?"**
> Chrome on desktop for now. If enough people here want Firefox, say so and I will look at
> it.

**"How is it different from RescueTime?"**
> RescueTime tracks your whole computer and is more powerful — but it is a paid
> subscription in dollars and it keeps your data on their servers. This one is browser-only,
> free, and keeps the data on your machine.
