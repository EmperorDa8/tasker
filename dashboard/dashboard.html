How this is decided

Tasker matches the sites and actions already in your local log against a catalogue of recognisable work tools. Each tool contributes to several kinds of work at once, weighted by how specific it is: a Kubernetes console says a lot, a search engine says nothing and is not counted at all.

Time enters through a square root, so one tool you live in cannot drown out three that each say something different, and a tool seen across many days counts for more than the same hours spent in one sitting.

Where the market runs one job under many names — AI Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, GenAI Engineer, LLM Engineer are the same tools in the same tabs — Tasker reports the family and lists the other titles alongside it. Splitting those into competing profiles would let a coin flip decide which title ended up next to your name. A specialism gets its own profile only where the tooling genuinely differs: vector stores for retrieval, eval harnesses for evaluation, attack tooling for red teaming.

When it refuses to answer

  • Under 45 minutes on recognised tools
  • Fewer than two kinds of tool
  • Fewer than four distinct sites
  • Fewer than three separate days
  • Two profiles scoring too closely — then it shows the shortlist instead of picking

Your own answer always wins. Set it in Settings and it replaces the inference everywhere, labelled as stated by you.
