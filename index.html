What Tasker does

Your work diary, written while you work.

No timers to start, no forms to fill in at 5pm. Tasker watches your active tab and turns the day into something you can actually read.

Automatic time tracking

Tasker tracks time on your active browser tab and files it into one of sixteen categories as you go — Development, AI, Finance, Travel, Health and more. It recognises over a thousand sites, and asks you about the ones it doesn't.

runs in the background · Manifest V3

A focus score you set the rules for

A daily score computed from how you actually browsed — and you decide what counts. Slide the weight on any category, or tell Tasker that LinkedIn is work for you. Hit Alt+Shift+L to log a win the moment it happens.

your job, your definition of productive

Reports you can actually send

Every day becomes a branded PDF — cover page, focus score, category charts, page numbers — and every month becomes a recap report with an AI-written summary, no API key or account to set up. Prefer plain text? Markdown is still one setting away.

PDF or markdown · yours forever

Sync to your own Drive

Connect your Google account and Tasker files logs into a folder in your Google Drive — via Google OAuth, only when you say so. No middleman, no mystery bucket.

your drive · your folder · your files

Grouped by repo and ticket

“6h on github.com” is not an answer. Tasker rolls the day up by what the time went into — dan/tasker — 1h 45m across PR #12, PR #15 and commits — so the monthly recap reads like a list of work, not a list of websites.

the answer to “what did you ship?”

Honest about what it can’t see

Tasker only sees Chrome. Time in your editor, on calls, in desktop apps is invisible to it — so it tells you: between 9:00 AM and 6:00 PM you were in Chrome for 3h; the other 6h went somewhere Tasker cannot see. A timesheet that quietly rounds down is worse than no timesheet.

browser time, labelled as browser time

A work profile that shows its working

From the tools you spend time in, Tasker works out what kind of work your week looks like — across 54 roles, including the ones that only exist since 2024: AI engineering, agent work, evals, MLOps, AI safety. Every finding comes with the evidence behind it: which tools, how many hours, over how many days.

It will also say “not enough evidence yet” and tell you what is missing, rather than guess. Where two profiles score too closely, it shows the shortlist instead of picking one. And where the market runs one job under six names — AI Engineer, GenAI Engineer, LLM Engineer — it names the family rather than pretending browsing can tell them apart. Disagree? Set it yourself and your answer wins everywhere.

Privacy, by architecture

Local-first. No backend. Nothing to leak.

A time tracker sees your whole day, so we built Tasker to keep it to itself. Your history lives in chrome.storage.local on your device and stays there — no account, no login, no analytics. Sync to your own Google Drive when you choose. The AI monthly summary sends category totals only ("Development: 58 hours"), never a single URL, page title or note. Your work profile is worked out on your machine and is never sent anywhere at all.

Your history stays putSites, page titles and times live in local browser storage and never leave it.
No selling, no sharingNo account needed to track, no analytics, no ads. Your browsing history is yours alone.
